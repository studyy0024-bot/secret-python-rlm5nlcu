# RealisticSnapshot-Zimage-Turbov5 Style Fingerprint

This fingerprint is based on the provided reference image/prompt set. Some reference numbers were duplicated or shifted, so the analysis is organized by visual pattern rather than numbering.

## Core DNA

The repeated style is raw contemporary smartphone realism: attractive social-media lifestyle subjects photographed in believable everyday locations with practical lighting, detailed surfaces, natural skin texture, and phone-camera imperfections.

The look is not studio fashion, not cinematic, and not glossy 8K. It is a phone-gallery realism style with strong environmental specificity.

## Repeated camera behavior

- Vertical frames dominate: 3:4, 4:5, and 9:16.
- Phone focal lengths are common: 24mm, 26mm, 28mm, or wide front-camera look.
- Arm-length selfies show slight wide-angle distortion and imperfect crop.
- Mirror selfies often place the phone over part of the face or torso.
- Friend-shot and social-media portraits use chest-height or slightly low/high casual phone angles.
- Close subjects are sharp with mild computational processing; backgrounds are either deep-focus or softly simulated portrait blur depending on scene.

## Lighting patterns

1. Harsh fluorescent indoor lighting
   - Parking garages, subway cars, utilitarian interiors.
   - Cold highlights, strong shadows, exposed tubes, concrete reflections.

2. Warm tungsten practical lighting
   - Restaurants, hotel rooms, indoor night scenes.
   - Amber drift, practical lamps, warm skin tones, visible high-ISO grain.

3. Direct phone flash
   - Night street portraits.
   - Bright subject, crushed black background, instant falloff, bloomed distant lights.

4. Soft window daylight
   - Bedrooms, mirror selfies, offices.
   - Gentle fill, white curtains, clean walls, shallow shadows.

5. Direct outdoor sun
   - Gardens, soccer fields, beach, vehicles at sunset.
   - Hard highlights, clipped hair/skin edges, punchy greens/blues, crisp ground shadows.

6. Overcast car-window light
   - Vehicle interiors.
   - Even facial light with darker cabin falloff and hazy wet exterior.

## Skin and human-detail language

Use natural human texture without airbrushing:

- visible pores
- faint sebaceous filaments
- subtle vellus hair
- oil or sweat sheen on forehead, nose, cheekbones, collarbones, shoulders, arms, legs
- soft specular highlights
- realistic tonal variation
- flyaways and individual hair strands
- makeup described physically when relevant: gloss, liner, lashes, blush, contour, catchlights

Skin should look real under the scene light, not plasticky or porcelain-perfect.

## Hair realism

Common hair treatments:

- center-parted straight hair with flyaways
- messy bedhead waves
- low bun with face-framing strands
- high ponytail tension
- damp braids or slick wet hair
- platinum-blonde hair catching warm practical light
- blunt bangs under flash
- motion softness at the ends

Hair should have individual strands, frizz, loose pieces, and scene-specific response to light.

## Clothing and material language

Prompts should describe actual fabric and texture:

- ribbed cotton
- cotton jersey
- nylon-spandex
- faux leather
- denim seams and stitching
- lace trim
- mesh chair texture
- cable knit
- athletic polyester
- bomber jacket nylon
- cotton dress folds
- metallic jewelry reflection
- phone-case surface texture

Garments should not be generic. Describe weave, sheen, stretch, wrinkles, tension, stitching, trim, logos only when requested, and material behavior under light.

## Environment construction rules

The environment must carry the realism. Avoid empty or generic backgrounds.

Useful detail types:

- garage: concrete pillars, pipes, ducts, signs, oil stains, tire marks, stall lines
- mirror selfie room: shelves, vents, curtains, desk, chair, clothing pile, bottles, wall art
- car interior: headrests, rear windows, wet street, cables, dark upholstery
- restaurant: table objects, napkins, cutlery, lamps, nearby diners, awnings, textured facade
- train: patterned seats, windows, station blur, fluorescent ceiling strips
- street night: parked cars, streetlights, black asphalt, underexposed houses
- outdoor daylight: foliage, grass blades, dog harness, soccer net, shadows, sand footprints
- sports/stadium: turf, seating tiers, railings, jersey fabric, distant signage

## Phone-camera artifact rules

Use scene-appropriate imperfections:

- mild wide-angle distortion
- digital sharpening
- phone HDR behavior
- slight compression smoothing
- high-ISO grain in dark areas
- mirror dust or smudges
- direct-flash falloff
- crushed blacks at night
- slight highlight clipping in sunlit areas
- portrait-mode softness only when appropriate
- imperfect framing and casual crop

## Negative prompt pattern

Avoid:

- cinematic lighting
- DSLR look
- studio photoshoot
- airbrushed skin
- plastic skin
- perfect symmetry
- glossy AI beauty
- fake bokeh
- over-clean background
- warped anatomy
- melted hands
- distorted vehicle parts
- unreadable logos
- unnatural reflections
- oversharpened render
- low-quality artifacts

## Prompt-writing rule

The best prompt structure is:

1. Subject and camera composition
2. Face, skin, hair, expression realism
3. Outfit, material, accessory details
4. Pose and action
5. Environment and background specificity
6. Lighting and atmosphere
7. Smartphone rendering and imperfections
8. Negative style line
